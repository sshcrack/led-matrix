CARRIER
191.6 x 191.6 x 16 mm. Rear web 4 mm, perimeter rib another 6 mm, four mounting bosses another 12 mm above the web. Four through bores 3.5 mm on the paper-confirmed 174 x 158 mm pattern.
- Eight more 3.5 mm holes, two on each edge, are provisional seam-joiner positions. They are symmetric, so the same carrier fits any of the four tiles. They are NOT panel mounting holes.
- Six 5 mm sight holes in the web sit over the drawing's intermediate inserts: one in the middle of each side edge, and two pairs 15 mm apart in the middle of the top and bottom edges. They are for looking through only, never for screws.
- A small notch in the top of the rib marks the carrier's "+Y" edge. Use it to describe orientation.
Carrier perimeter/body size and 12 mm spacer height remain concept assumptions to be tested.

The +16 mm boss faces touch the panel insert faces. The rear web sits 12 mm behind those faces; the printed rear base is 16 mm behind. Screw heads would remain outside the rear base. This is a mounting/interference trial, not finished carrier-to-frame, wall-mount or fabric-clamp hardware. A complete display must not be hung from this trial part.

SCREW TEST COUPON
80 x 24 x 10 mm, notched corner at the left of the top row. Hold it with the notch top-left.
- Top row, clearance holes (screw should slide through freely): 3.2 / 3.4 / 3.5 / 3.6 mm. Record the smallest one an M3 screw passes freely.
- Bottom row, pilot holes (screw cuts its own thread into the plastic), from the left: 2.4 / 2.6 / 2.8 mm for M3, then 2.0 / 2.2 mm for M2.5. Screw an M3 x 12 into each M3 hole and an M2.5 x 6 into each M2.5 hole by hand. Record for each: too tight to turn / good (firm, holds when you pull) / too loose (strips or wobbles). Then take the good one out and in again three times: does it still hold? These sizes go into the plates, the Pi mounts and the rear cover.
- Bottom right: an M3 nut trap (5.8 mm across flats, 2.5 mm deep) on top of a 3.4 mm hole. Press an M3 nut in: record whether it goes in by finger, needs a light tap, or does not fit, and whether it then stays when you turn a screw into it from below. The carriers will be joined this way.

HALO CHANNEL COUPON
38.5 x 70 x 6.1 mm, three channels for the halo LED strip (owner's strip: 10.0 x 2.63 mm). Inner widths 10.4 / 10.7 / 11.0 mm, marked by 1 / 2 / 3 small notches at one end; 3.3 mm inner height; 1.2 mm lips leave an 8.0-8.6 mm opening for the LEDs. Cut a piece of strip two LEDs long at its cut marks and slide it in from the end of each channel, LEDs facing out between the lips. Record which channel holds it without squeezing or rattling, and whether the lips stayed straight (no sag onto the strip).

DC SOCKET COUPON
40 x 26 x 6 mm wall section for the Kycon KPJX-PM-4S panel socket: 14 mm body cutout (assumed from the drawing), two 2.8 mm holes on 19 mm for M2.5 screws, 1 mm flange pocket on the outside face. Only test it once the socket has arrived: the flange sits in the pocket, two M2.5 x 12 screws from the other side into the flange. Record whether the body fits through, the flange sits flat, and the brick's plug clicks in and holds.

PIXEL GRID COUPON
49.2 x 49.2 mm, 16 x 16 square cells on the panel's 3 mm LED pitch, 0.8 mm walls. The left 8 columns are 3 mm deep, the right 8 columns 6 mm deep. Black filament gives the darkest gaps; if the job is one colour, use what the carrier is printed in. To test: lay it on a running panel so each LED sits in the middle of a cell (the walls rest on the LED tips over the gaps between LEDs). Look at it bare, then with baking paper or printer paper on top of each half. Compare with the panel bare and with your other samples, from the normal viewing distance, with a dark scene and with the panel off. Record which depth looks better and whether the pixels look like separate squares.

CABLE TUNNEL GAUGE
15 x 79 mm, three tunnels 23 mm wide (a HUB75 ribbon is about 21 mm): 6 mm high (= the space under the carrier's 4 mm outer rim), 9 mm, and 12 mm (= the space under the carrier's web band, the 16 mm wide flat edge). Lay a cable on the panel back exactly as it would run where it crosses a carrier edge or the seam between two panels: the HUB75 ribbon between neighbouring panels with its fold, and the power cable. Slide the gauge over it. Record the lowest tunnel it passes through without being squashed, for each cable. Also try the plugged-in HUB75 plug and the power plug with its cable bend under the 12 mm tunnel. This tells us where the next carrier needs cable notches.

PHYSICAL CHECK (no screws, no pressure)
0. Unplug the panel. Unscrew and set aside ALL EIGHT magnetic feet: the four corners AND the four intermediate ones. Leave every insert empty. A foot left in an intermediate insert reaches the carrier's web and stops it seating. The panels may stay glued together.
1. Flatness first: put the carrier bosses-down on something flat (glass, a stone worktop). Do not press. Try to slide a sheet of printer paper (about 0.1 mm) under each boss. Record which bosses a sheet slides under. This shows print warp before the panel is involved.
2. Turn the carrier over, bosses toward the panel, and lay it on the panel's back so the four corner bores line up with the four corner inserts. Let it rest under its own weight only. Never press, clamp or force it: a light frame bends under hand pressure and hides gaps.
3. Alignment: push a 3 mm drill bit (smooth end first) or a 3 mm rod through each corner bore to the insert. Record whether it drops in centred at all four corners.
4. Gaps: with the carrier resting, try to slide a paper sheet between each boss and its insert. Record each corner (top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right, with the notch at the top). Note anything the carrier touches instead of the inserts: PCB parts, plastic ribs, glue, HUB75 ribbons or power wiring.
5. Contact: colour the four boss tops with a washable marker, rest the carrier again, lift it straight off and photograph where the marker transferred (insert face, or the frame around it). Keep photos private.
6. Sight holes: look through the six 5 mm holes. Record which ones show an insert centred in the hole. The left and right side holes should each show one. At the top and bottom edges only one hole of each pair should; which one tells us which way round the drawing is.
7. Cables: for each connector and each cable that has to cross the carrier's outline, note where it crosses (the outer 16 mm web band, the 4 mm rib, or the open middle) and roughly how far above the insert faces it sits. Check the intended cable bends in the open middle. In particular: can the HUB75 ribbon between two panels fold flat under the carrier's outer rib (6 mm above the panel frame), or does it have to loop over the carrier's back?
8. Measure with calipers, if you have them: one module's outside body size in both directions; whether neighbouring module bodies touch; and the distance spanning 10 LED pitches across a seam compared with 10 pitches inside one module, in both directions. Also the distance from LED tip to insert face on each module.
9. Test the screw test coupon (and the other coupons, see above). Confirm a known matching thread before trying screws in panel inserts. A 5.2 mm smooth foot shaft does not identify the thread. Do not use a guessed screw length or tighten into a blind stop. The carrier's 16 mm grip thickness is not the screw length: usable insert engagement and head/washer details are still unknown. Final length will be selected after this check. If a screw is tried later, finger-snug only.
10. Mark which edge has HUB75 IN, relative to the notch.

REPORT
Flatness per boss (step 1), pin alignment per corner (3), paper gaps per corner (4), contact photos (5, private), which sight holes show inserts (6), the cable table and ribbon crossing (7), measurements (8), screw test coupon: clearance hole, M3 and M2.5 pilot results, nut trap (9), HUB75 IN edge (10), the halo channel that fits, the pixel grid look (which depth, with which diffuser, or none), the lowest tunnel each cable passes, and later the DC socket fit.
